# "What If You Could See Who Has a Crush on You?"
## Full 5-Chapter Storytelling Master Script (JeremyBearimyTheory / What If Style)

---

### CHAPTER 1: Cold Open, The 3 Rules & The Hallway Test
**Word Count:** 235 words  
**Setting:** Bedroom morning -> City Sidewalk -> School Hallway (dusk perspective)

Imagine waking up on a regular Monday morning, opening your front door, and suddenly noticing bright pink numbers glowing over everyone’s heads. At first, you think you are just hallucinating from lack of sleep, but as you walk down the sidewalk toward school, three impossible rules quickly become crystal clear.

Rule number one: every human being now displays a live percentage from zero to one hundred, showing exactly how much romantic interest they feel toward the person they are looking at.
Rule number two: the meter updates instantly based on involuntary neurological chemistry, reacting to pupil dilation, subconscious vocal shifts, and sudden spikes of dopamine that nobody can fake.
And rule number three: if two people ever reach ninety-five percent or higher, a glowing neon-pink thread physically manifests between their chests, locking them into an unmistakable connection.

When you push through the double doors into the crowded school hallway, the numbers are completely overwhelming. You turn to your best friend Mo, and right above his curly hair sits a bright blue zero percent with the subtitle Pure Bro Zone. You glance over at Maya, the most popular girl in your grade, and her meter sits at a predictable three percent. But then you spot two notoriously shy classmates standing near the slate lockers, secretly staring across the corridor with matching eighty-six percent meters glowing above their heads, totally oblivious that the other person feels the exact same way.

### CHAPTER 2: Classroom & Cafeteria Experiments
**Word Count:** 215 words  
**Setting:** Classroom (with dynamic board) -> School Cafeteria (lunch tables & tray counter)

Second period arrives, and you find yourself sitting directly across from Maya at the wooden lab tables in science class. For three years, you have wondered what she thought of you, and right now her floating pink meter sits at a humble fourteen percent. Naturally, you try to do what every nervous teenager does: you puff out your chest, lean back against your chair, and casually try to brag about your weekend. But the moment you start acting like an arrogant know-it-all, you watch in horror as Maya’s percentage instantly plunges from fourteen down to six percent.

Panicking at lunch in the cafeteria, you accidentally knock your metal fork off the table, fumble trying to catch it mid-air, and end up laughing at your own ridiculous clumsy mistake. Maya glances over, giggles at your genuine reaction, and her pink meter suddenly skyrockets from six all the way up to thirty-eight percent.

Before you can even celebrate, you notice your friend Mo staring miserably at his phone. You look across the cafeteria and spot a girl at the corner table watching Mo with a massive seventy-four percent meter pulsing over her hair. You nudge Mo, tell him exactly what to say, and watch his jaw drop as her score climbs with every word.

### CHAPTER 3: The Real Psychology Twist
**Word Count:** 205 words  
**Setting:** Modern Office / Technical Psychology Blueprint Diagrams

Watching these floating numbers bounce around the cafeteria reveals three profound psychological truths about human attraction that scientists have documented for decades.

The first breakthrough is called the Pratfall Effect, proven in nineteen sixty-six by social psychologist Elliot Aronson. His landmark experiments showed that highly competent people who make a small clumsy mistake, like spilling a drop of coffee, are perceived as significantly more likable and attractive than people who try to project robotic perfection. Your clumsy fork fumble did not ruin your chances; it proved you were relatable.

The second discovery is known as the Liking Gap, published in twenty-eighteen by researchers at Harvard and Cornell. Studies proved that after meeting someone new, people consistently underestimate how much the other person actually enjoyed their conversation by an average of twenty percent. Our internal social anxiety convinces us that we were awkward, even when the other person walked away thinking we were wonderful.

The third mechanism is Reciprocal Liking. Decades of behavioral research prove that one of the strongest predictors of whether someone likes you is simply learning that you already like them. Finding out someone finds you interesting releases internal inhibition, making your own subconscious warmth surge in return.

### CHAPTER 4: The 99% Mystery Thread
**Word Count:** 195 words  
**Setting:** School Hallway Dusk -> Stairwell Interior

By the time the final bell rings and students flood out toward the parking lot, you feel like you have finally unlocked the secret code to teenage social dynamics. But as you step back into the empty stairwell to grab your backpack from your locker, your chest suddenly lights up with intense warmth.

You look down, and your heart nearly stops. Stretching straight out from the center of your chest is a blazing, shimmering neon-pink thread, humming with electric light in the dim staircase. Remember the third rule: this luminous thread only activates when someone’s crush score reaches an unbelievable ninety-five percent or higher.

You frantically look around the hallway, but nobody is there. The glowing thread angles sharply upward, winding through the iron banister and leading straight up the concrete steps toward the roof access door. Your pulse pounds in your ears. Who could possibly have a ninety-nine percent score on you? Could Maya have experienced a massive delayed spike? Or did someone you barely know carry a secret that deep? Desperate for answers, you sprint up the stairs two steps at a time, following the neon filament into the dusk.

---

### CHAPTER 5: The Rooftop Reveal & Ending
**Word Count:** 240 words  
**Setting:** Sunset Rooftop with Water Tower & Golden Horizon

You push open the heavy metal rooftop door and step out into the crisp evening air, where the low golden sun is setting across the city skyline. The pink thread stretches straight across the gravel roof, leading directly to the edge of the parapet.

Standing there with her hands in her pockets is the quiet girl from your homeroom class. She is the girl who always sat in the back row, wore oversized teal hoodies, and constantly looked down at her shoes whenever you walked past her desk. For months, you assumed she found you annoying or disliked you because she never said a single word.

But floating directly above her head is a massive, pulsing pink heart displaying a score of ninety-nine percent. In that quiet sunset moment, you realize the greatest irony of all: The Shyness Paradox. She was never ignoring you out of cold indifference; she was looking away because her feelings were so overwhelmingly intense that she could barely breathe whenever you were near.

You take a deep breath, walk across the gravel, and gently say, "Hey." Her cheeks turn bright crimson, she looks up into your eyes, and with a soft chime and a brilliant golden sparkle, her meter clicks to one hundred percent before slowly dissolving into the sunset.
